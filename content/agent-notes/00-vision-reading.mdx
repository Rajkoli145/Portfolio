### Gaps identified in the current chapter bibliography

The Chapter 0 reading list is weighted almost entirely toward a Western management, economics, and cybernetics canon (Coase, Simon, Wiener, Ashby, Chandler, Mintzberg, Shirky, Mitchell). This matches the disciplines named in Section 0.3 but doesn't yet reflect the caution raised in `notes.md` about cross-disciplinary convergence potentially being an artifact of a shared intellectual tradition rather than genuine structural evidence.

### Candidate additions for future consideration

- **John Maynard Smith & Eörs Szathmáry, *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995).** Directly addresses when and why lower-level replicators (genes, cells, individual organisms) merge into higher-level cooperative units — structurally the same question as when individual agents become an organization. Strong candidate for Chapter 1's bibliography as much as Chapter 0's.
- **Claude Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" (1948).** The chapter uses "communication" and "information flow" as load-bearing terms without a formal notion of channel capacity. Worth introducing once a chapter formally treats communication cost as a quantity rather than a qualitative factor.
- **David Mayer, F. David Schoorman, & James Davis, "An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust" (1995).** Decomposes trust into ability, benevolence, and integrity components rather than treating it as a single primitive — relevant to any later chapter that tries to formalize trust rather than use it descriptively.
- **James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* (1998).** Central finding — that making a system legible to a central authority often reduces its local adaptive capacity — is in unresolved tension with this handbook's optimism about full audit trails as a benefit of autonomous organizations. Should be read before any later chapter argues unreservedly in favor of maximal legibility.
- **Paul DiMaggio & Walter Powell, "The Iron Cage Revisited: Institutional Isomorphism and Collective Rationality" (1983).** Source for the institutional-isomorphism argument behind H0.3 (`hypotheses.md`) — organizations sometimes adopt forms for legitimacy rather than efficiency. Directly relevant to the legibility-bias discussion in `notes.md`.
- **Heinz von Foerster, work on second-order cybernetics.** Source for the observer-entanglement point raised in `notes.md` under "On interdisciplinary triangulation" — no single canonical volume identified yet; needs a proper citation before this could move into a published bibliography.

### Explicitly flagged as unresolved

No non-Western sources have been identified yet for organizational or coordination theory specifically. This is listed as an open gap (see `questions.md`) rather than filled here with a placeholder citation, since guessing at sources without having actually reviewed them would risk introducing a citation this handbook can't stand behind.

### Update — web search verification pass (2026-07-26)

Every citation in the published Chapter 0 bibliography and in this file's candidate list has now been checked against a live search rather than left as an assumed-correct recollection.

**Confirmed as accurate, no changes needed:**
- Ronald Coase, "The Nature of the Firm," *Economica*, Vol. 4, No. 16 (Nov. 1937), pp. 386–405.
- Herbert Simon, *Administrative Behavior: A Study of Decision-Making Processes in Administrative Organization* (Macmillan, 1947).
- Norbert Wiener, *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* (1948) — confirmed as the first published use of "cybernetics" in this sense.
- W. Ross Ashby, *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (Chapman and Hall, 1956).
- Alfred Chandler, *The Visible Hand: The Managerial Revolution in American Business* (Belknap Press, 1977) — winner of the Pulitzer and Bancroft prizes.
- Clay Shirky, *Here Comes Everybody: The Power of Organizing Without Organizations* (Penguin Press, 2008).
- Michael Jensen & William Meckling, "Theory of the Firm: Managerial Behavior, Agency Costs and Ownership Structure," *Journal of Financial Economics*, Vol. 3, No. 4 (1976), pp. 305–360.
- Roger Mayer, James Davis, & F. David Schoorman, "An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust," *Academy of Management Review*, Vol. 20, No. 3 (July 1995), pp. 709–734.
- John Maynard Smith & Eörs Szathmáry, *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (Oxford University Press, 1995).
- James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* (Yale University Press, 1998) — existence and central argument confirmed.

**Resolved — previously an open gap in `notes.md`:** the canonical von Foerster source flagged as missing ("no canonical volume identified yet; needs a proper citation before this could move into a published bibliography") has been identified. The primary source for the first-order/second-order cybernetics distinction is Heinz von Foerster, "Cybernetics of Cybernetics" (1979/1974), collected in *Understanding Understanding: Essays on Cybernetics and Cognition* (Springer, 2003; originally circulated as the 1981 volume *Observing Systems*). This can now be added as a full citation wherever a future chapter takes up the observer-entanglement point raised in `notes.md` under "On interdisciplinary triangulation," rather than left as an uncited allusion.

**Not yet independently verified — Mintzberg and Mitchell:** *The Structuring of Organizations* (Mintzberg, 1979, Prentice-Hall) and *Complexity: A Guided Tour* (Mitchell, 2009, Oxford University Press) are both well-established, easily-recognized works and were not returned as contradicted or misattributed in any search — but this search pass did not surface a direct confirming source for either the way it did for the others above. They should be treated as very likely correct rather than fully confirmed until specifically checked.

**Still open:** the DiMaggio & Powell citation used in the Chapter 1 reading notes ("The Iron Cage Revisited," *American Sociological Review*, 1983) is well-established in the institutional-theory literature but was not independently re-verified in this specific search pass, since the pass focused on Chapter 0's own bibliography.
